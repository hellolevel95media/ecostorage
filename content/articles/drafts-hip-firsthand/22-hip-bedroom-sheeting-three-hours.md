Before HIP started, we searched online for how to protect our things. The advice was consistent: wrap everything in plastic, tape the seams, and the dust stays out. We believed it. After the works, we were far less sure.

This article is our account, with photos. It is not a claim about every HIP project, but the way the sheeting failed is easy to understand, and it changes what is worth doing in advance.

## Three hours of careful wrapping

The master bedroom was being prepared for works in its en-suite toilet. The bed went under plastic sheeting reinforced with masking tape. Belongings were huddled beside the cabinets, and sheeting was taped over everything, with the top, sides and bottom all sealed. Floor padding ran through the room into the toilet.

[[photo: unit conditions/bedroom.jpeg | Master bedroom wrapped ahead of en-suite toilet works. Our flat during HIP.]]

All of this took almost three hours. By the end it looked meticulous, and we felt well prepared.

## What the dust did

The dust got in. Fine dust reached the mattress, the sealed cabinets and the belongings inside them. The dining table was wrapped twice in plastic sheet, and when we unwrapped it, the table was covered in a thick layer of fine dust.

The reason is time. HIP drilling in a block does not last a day or two. It goes on across months, because the crew moves from unit to unit, and the constant vibration loosens tape seals quickly. A seal that was airtight on a Saturday morning does not stay that way through weeks of drilling.

The contractors' own sheeting has similar limits. It is a thicker, stiffer grey material than the plastic households buy online. For access to a room, they often cut an opening and seal it with a flap that works as a door for people. At floor level, the gap still lets dust move into the room.

[[photo: unit conditions/livingroom.jpeg | Contractor sheeting with a cut flap used as a door. Our flat during HIP.]]

The large dust extractor the contractors run helps with bigger particles. It did not stop a fine, misty dust that hung in the living room and became obvious whenever the ceiling lights went on. More on that fog is in the [HIP timeline article](/resources/hip-timeline-week-by-week).

[[photo: unit conditions/vaccum.jpeg | Contractor dust extractor with ducting behind plastic. Our flat during HIP.]]

## What the cleanup actually took

The rooms next to the toilets, the master bedroom and the living room, were the worst. Cleaning followed this pattern:

- **Vacuuming first, then giving up on it.** Home vacuums of the Dyson type could not cope with the volume of dust, so we switched to wiping with rags and mopping.
- **Many passes to get the floors right.** Dust on a floor feels slippery underfoot, and feet are sensitive to it, so it was obvious when a floor was still not clean.
- **Dust kept resurfacing.** In corners and on objects that a general mop pass did not reach, dust appeared again days later.
- **Fabrics varied.** Most could be washed. Silk items had to be thrown out, and a carpet went too, because no amount of vacuuming or dusting removed the dust.

Altogether, about two weeks of cleaning after the works finished made the flat feel liveable again. Dust also turned up in odd places long afterwards. One example was the back of the fridge, covered in a thick layer of dust mixed with whatever had been there before.

## The smarter plan

Looking back, we would put effort somewhere else. Taping and wrapping buys some protection, but storing items removes the problem. The glass display shelving and some living-room furniture went into EcoStorage storage for the HIP period, and none of it needed cleaning afterwards.

[[photo: storage/_blurred/photo_1_2026-11-20_13-33-23.jpg | Glass display shelving stretch-wrapped on a pallet in EcoStorage storage for the HIP period.]]

The other principle is to keep floors bare. With little on the floor, a final mop and wipe of the whole unit takes one to two hours. Cleaning item by item as problems appear, spread across months, is more tiring in every way. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does plastic sheeting keep HIP dust out?
In our flat it did not. Fine dust reached the mattress, sealed cabinets and a dining table that had been wrapped twice, because drilling over months loosened the tape seals.

### How long did cleaning take after HIP?
About two weeks of wiping and mopping made the flat liveable again, and dust kept turning up in corners and behind appliances after that.

### Which items could not be saved?
Silk items and a carpet were thrown out. Other fabrics could be washed.

### What is the best way to protect belongings during HIP?
Storing them. Items stored away from the flat avoided the dust completely, and bare floors made the final cleanup much faster.

## Summary

- Almost three hours of wrapping did not stop fine dust reaching the mattress, cabinets or a doubly wrapped table.
- Months of drilling loosen tape seals, and contractor sheeting leaves gaps at doors.
- Cleanup took about two weeks, and silk items and a carpet were lost.
- Storing belongings and keeping floors bare is quicker than cleaning item by item.
