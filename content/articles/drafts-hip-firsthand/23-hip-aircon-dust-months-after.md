The contractors in our flat wrapped the aircon units in plastic before works began, as part of their routine protection. It looked like enough. When we switched two of the units on after HIP, anyone sitting in those rooms started coughing and sneezing.

This is our experience, not a rule for every HIP project. The reason it happened is easy to explain, and the fix is a short list.

## What it felt like

The two units that were badly affected were in rooms that sat close to the dust. The moment they ran, they pushed out fine particles that irritated the nose and throat. It was not a faint smell. People in the room reacted straight away.

Nothing on the outside of the units looked wrong. The front panels were clean, and the problem was inside.

The dust kept coming for weeks. We seldom use aircon at home, so the units had little chance to purge themselves, and the dust took much longer to clear than it would in a flat where the aircon runs every day.

## Why it happens

An indoor aircon unit pulls room air across its filter and coil, then blows it back out. During HIP, a block can be full of fine dust for weeks, and plastic sheeting only slows it down. Drilling across months loosens tape seals, which is why a table wrapped twice in our flat still ended up under a thick layer of dust.

[[photo: misc/tidy.jpeg | Furniture consolidated and covered before works. Our flat during HIP.]]

Dust that gets past the wrapping collects in the filter and on the blower. When the unit runs, it is released into the room. There is a second effect too. Removing the plastic at the end of the works can shake settled dust loose near the unit.

For how much dust the works produce, see [How Much Dust Does HIP Really Generate?](/resources/how-much-dust-hip-generates).

## What fixed it

We called for aircon servicing. The technician washed the filters and flushed the blower, which removed the dust that had built up inside the units.

Then they added a step that is easy to miss. After the servicing, they ran the units for an entire day while nobody was home. An aircon unit traps and filters other particles in the room as it runs, so a long empty run helped pick up dust that cleaning had not reached.

The order matters. Clean the rooms first, service the units, then run them. Servicing a unit in a room that is still dusty invites the dust back into the filter.

## A short plan for your own flat

- **Book the aircon service for after the works.** Treat it as part of the HIP schedule, and ask for the filters to be washed and the blower flushed.
- **Clean the rooms first.** Wipe and mop before the technician arrives.
- **Run each unit while the flat is empty.** One long run on the first day lets the unit trap what is still in the air.
- **Keep an eye on filters.** Check them again after the first few weeks.
- **Be careful with sleeping rooms.** Anyone with asthma or allergies should avoid sleeping under a unit that has not been serviced.

## Reduce what the dust can reach

Fine dust finds everything, so the more you remove from the flat, the less there is to clean. In our flat, the glass display shelving and some living-room furniture went into EcoStorage storage for the HIP period.
