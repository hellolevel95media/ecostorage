HIP brings workers, materials and noise to a block, and it also brings the curious. In our HDB flat, the first uninvited visitor walked in within the first week of works. Over the whole period, two strangers entered the home and had to be asked to leave.

None of it was violent, but it was unsettling, and it shaped how we think about who stays home and what stays in the flat.

## Why the door is open

During HIP, front doors stay unlocked so workers can come and go as they need to. That is a practical requirement of the works, and the contractors in our block could not change it. When we raised the strangers with them, the contractors could do little, because an unlocked door is part of how the job is done.

In our flat, a plastic board served as the temporary door after the old wooden door came out, and it stood in place until the new door was fitted. It stops draughts and dust. It does not stop anyone from pushing in.

[[photo: unit conditions/door.jpeg | Plastic board used as a temporary door while the new door awaits installation. Our flat during HIP.]]

Earlier in the works, the wooden door had a plastic protective board with a cutout for the lock.

## The notice

Many residents put up a notice. Our flat had a handwritten one saying nobody should enter unless they were HIP workers, and that there was CCTV inside.

[[photo: misc/notice.jpeg | Handwritten no-entry notice with CCTV warning. Our flat during HIP.]]

Notices like this are common because people from outside the block sometimes walk up to see the works and wander into open flats. In this case, it did not stop anyone. Two strangers walked in, and the family chased them out.

## Why HDB wants an adult at home

HDB's guidance requires an adult household member to be in the flat during the working hours of the works. We saw the practical reason. That person supervises the works and also notices who is in the home. A stranger is much less likely to wander through a flat with someone sitting in it.

The cost is real. The person on supervision duty has to sit in an environment filled with dust and drilling. In our flat, the supervising adult could not stay in the living room for more than about half an hour before heading to the lift landing for fresh air.

We handled it by sharing the work. Parents and a younger sibling stayed with the grandparents for the first week, and family members rotated daytime shifts to supervise the flat. For other options, see [HIP When Nobody Is Home All Day](/resources/hip-when-nobody-is-home-all-day).

## Protecting what is in the flat

Because the door cannot be locked, the practical advice is about what you leave behind:

- **Keep small valuables out.** Watches, jewellery, cash and documents are better off with a friend or relative than in a drawer in a flat with an open door. A storage service is one option, but a trusted home works too.
- **Store bulky valuables.** In our flat, the glass display shelving and some living-room furniture went into EcoStorage storage for the HIP period, so there was less in the flat to guard and less to worry about.

[[photo: storage/_blurred/photo_2_2026-11-20_13-33-23.jpg | Wrapped cabinet on a tagged pallet in EcoStorage storage during HIP.]]

- **Do not leave someone alone who cannot handle a confrontation.** A child or a frail relative should call another adult, not deal with a stranger.
- **Tell your neighbours.** People next door often notice unfamiliar faces first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do strangers really walk into flats during HIP?
In our flat, the first stranger walked in within the first week, and two did during the works, despite a no-entry notice.

### Why can't I lock my door during HIP?
Front doors stay unlocked so workers can come and go as they need to, and the contractors cannot change that.

### Why does HDB want an adult at home during the works?
The adult supervises the works and is a presence in the flat, which makes it less likely that a stranger will wander in.

### What should I move out of the flat before HIP?
Small valuables such as watches, jewellery, cash and documents, and bulky items that are expensive or hard to protect.

## Summary

- One stranger walked into a HIP flat within the first week, and two over the works.
- Doors stay unlocked for the workers, so a notice may not be enough.
- The supervising adult's shift in the dust and noise is hard, so share it if you can.
- Keep valuables with friends or relatives, or in storage.
