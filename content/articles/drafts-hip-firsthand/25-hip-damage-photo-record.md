HIP happens in occupied homes, so some damage is a realistic possibility. In our HDB flat, three things went wrong: the kitchen tiles cracked, a waterproofing leak appeared, and a pair of glasses was crushed. The contractors dealt with the first two quickly, and the third was our own accident.

Our overall verdict was positive. The way the contractors responded mattered more than the problems themselves. This article is that flat's account, to show what to expect and what to photograph. For the formal process, see [HIP Defects and Damaged Belongings](/resources/hip-defects-damaged-belongings).

## The cracked kitchen tiles

A resident found the damage while heading to the kitchen to fetch a glass of water. Constant drilling inside the unit and in the units above and below had cracked the kitchen floor tiles. HIP does not include kitchen remodelling, so the tiles were not part of the planned works. The damage came from the works themselves.

[[photo: damage/3.jpeg | Cracked kitchen tiles seen toward the kitchen toilet. Our flat during HIP.]]

The response was fast. The contractors called over a supervisor straight away and began the repair process. They asked whether they could dig up and replace a larger area than the cracked patch, in case the tiles around it were already cracked in ways that did not yet show. We agreed. We are not certain of every step of the repair, such as which materials went underneath, because the kitchen was so close to the toilet being hacked that it was dusty and poorly ventilated, and staying in it was unpleasant. Because the final inspection included the tile area, we count the repair as taking a full month.

[[photo: damage/4.jpeg | Close-up of a cracked tile edge and debris. Our flat during HIP.]]

## The leak that the contractors found

During the contractors' testing of the new toilet, a leak showed up where the waterproofing had been done poorly. We do not know exactly which test method was used, but the crew pointed out the problem on their own, said they would fix it, and did. We understood that a sound waterproofing result is a passing requirement for the final inspection, and the repair was done by that point.

[[photo: damage/6.jpeg | Leak being plugged in the late stages of toilet works. Our flat during HIP.]]

We credited the contractors for raising an issue they had not known about. For how the official water test works, see [HIP Toilet Water Test](/resources/hip-toilet-water-test-opt-out).

## A pair of glasses on the floor

The last photo is of a pair of glasses that a contractor stepped on. A family member had left them on the floor, and in a flat with workers walking through all day, anything at floor level will get trodden on. The contractor offered to make restitution, and we turned it down because accidents happen.

[[photo: damage/1.jpeg | Glasses damaged on the floor during works. Our flat during HIP.]]

The lesson is simple. Anything small and fragile belongs in a drawer or a bag, in a room that stays closed.

## What to photograph and what to move

From our flat, a few habits are worth copying:

- **Photograph the flat before works begin.** Floors, tiles, walls, furniture and fixtures. Dated photos of the starting condition remove arguments about what was already there.
- **Photograph damage straight away.** Use a wide shot and a close-up from more than one angle, as the tile photos above do.
- **Raise it with the supervisor early.** In our flat, the response was immediate. HDB's defect liability period is one year, and the booklet gives the reporting channel.
- **Move fragile pieces out of the flat.** In our flat, the glass display shelving went into EcoStorage storage for the HIP period, so the most breakable furniture was nowhere near the drilling.

Some of the other furniture was stored too, on the suggestion of the contractors' inspection crew, who recommended moving a few pieces that would otherwise be in the workers' way. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can HIP drilling crack kitchen tiles?
In our flat, drilling in the unit and the units above and below cracked the kitchen floor tiles. The contractors repaired them, and we count the repair as taking a full month, because the final inspection covered the tile area.

### How did the contractors handle the tile damage?
They called a supervisor straight away and asked to replace a wider area than the cracked tiles, in case the surrounding tiles were cracked too.

### What happens if the waterproofing leaks?
In our flat, the contractors found a leak during their own testing, said they would fix it, and completed the repair by the final inspection.

### What should I photograph before HIP?
Floors, tiles, walls, furniture and fixtures, so there is a dated record of the starting condition.

## Summary

- Drilling cracked kitchen tiles in one HIP flat, and the contractors replaced a wider area than the visible damage.
- A waterproofing leak was found by the contractors' own testing and fixed before the final inspection.
- Glasses left on the floor were crushed, so keep small fragile items out of reach.
- Photograph the flat before works, and raise any damage with the supervisor immediately.
