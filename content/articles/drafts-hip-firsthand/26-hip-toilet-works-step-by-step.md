The toilet is the centrepiece of HIP. It is the room the contractors take apart, and the one that changes most when they leave. These photos follow one toilet through the process in an HDB flat that went through HIP.

The stages can look alarming while they happen, so it helps to know what each one is. Our overall judgement, which comes at the end, was that the work was done well.

## Stage 1: hacking down to bare brick

The contractors hack the entire toilet down to raw brick. Almost everything is removed, including the toilet roll holder, the old sink, any cabinetry, electric or gas water heaters, hanging racks, the toilet bowl, fans and ceiling lights.

[[photo: toilet works/toilet hacking/bare1.jpeg | Toilet hacked down to raw brick. Our flat during HIP.]]

Two things stay. The original plumbing piping is left untouched, and the contractors hack around the original window frame so it remains in place.

[[photo: toilet works/toilet hacking/windowframe.jpeg | Window frame preserved while the wall around it is hacked. Our flat during HIP.]]

This is the dustiest and loudest stage, and in our flat it was the worst part of the whole experience. Contractors do one toilet at a time, so the dust from one room overlaps with the next. Even with the large extractor, a persistent fog of fine dust built up in the living room. It was easy to see when the ceiling lights went on, and it stung the eyes, nose and throat. For how this fits into the full schedule, see [HIP Timeline: What to Expect Week by Week](/resources/hip-timeline-week-by-week).

## Stage 2: waterproofing

A layer of waterproofing membrane goes onto the toilet floor, followed by a thicker second layer.

[[photo: toilet works/toilet hacking/waterproofing2.jpeg | Thicker second waterproofing layer on the floor. Our flat during HIP.]]

The fresh layer dries in the open air over one to three days, sometimes with an electric fan. The smell is strong during that time and affects anyone living in the unit.

## Stage 3: concrete screed

The contractors concrete the floor over the waterproofing, and the walls, to create a smooth surface for tiling.

[[photo: toilet works/toilet hacking/concrete.jpeg | Concreted floor ready for tiling. Our flat during HIP.]]

A first water test takes place before the floor is covered. For what the test involves and what happens if the floor fails, see [HIP Toilet Water Test](/resources/hip-toilet-water-test-opt-out). In our flat, the contractors' own testing also caught a leak that we had not known about, and it was fixed by the final inspection.

## Stage 4: tiling

Tilers use a laser level to mark guide lines, then apply the wall tiles and level the floor so the grout lines align. The outer wall of the toilet is retiled with matching tiles after being hacked bare.

[[photo: toilet works/toilet hacking/tiling.jpeg | Wall tiles being applied along laser guide lines. Our flat during HIP.]]

Site staff from the contractor inspect the work at the end of each stage before the next one begins.

## Stage 5: fittings and inspection

New fittings go in, including a pedestal pan and a basin, and a fall-assist grab bar is installed as part of the upgrade.

[[photo: toilet works/toilet.jpeg | Finished toilet with a fall-assist grab bar. Our flat during HIP.]]

Just before final inspection, the plastic is still on the ceramic fittings and the floor is dirty with installation remnants. After the inspection, the plastic comes off, and the floor can be wet from the final water test.

[[photo: toilet works/common_toilet_fittings.jpeg | Finished toilet after final inspection, floor wet from the water test. Our flat during HIP.]]

## Our verdict

The toilet work was good. Judged as if we had been paying customers, we got our money's worth. The floor tiles are textured and grippy, which gives better support for elderly residents and young children. The crew was responsive throughout, and a dedicated customer support team had set up an office at the ground floor of the block, using part of the void deck. Residents could walk in to speak to someone, or message them on WhatsApp when the team was out inspecting units.

## Living through the toilet works

Two things are worth planning for. The first week of hacking is the hardest, and many households choose to stay elsewhere for it. In our flat, family members moved in with in-laws, partners and grandparents, and the person who stayed behind to supervise rotated shifts. A temporary toilet was placed in the kitchen, sealed with plastic sheeting, for residents who stayed.

Anything you can remove before the hacking starts is one less item to protect. In our flat, the glass display shelving and some living-room furniture went into EcoStorage storage for the HIP period. ## Frequently asked questions

### What happens to the toilet during HIP?
The contractors hack it down to bare brick, waterproof the floor in two layers, concrete the floor and walls, then tile and fit new fixtures.

### What stays in the toilet when it is hacked?
In our flat, the original plumbing piping and the window frame were kept, and nearly everything else was removed.

### How long does the waterproofing smell last?
The fresh layer dries over one to three days, and the smell is strong during that time.

### Was the finished toilet worth it?
We felt it was, citing the quality of the work, the grippy floor tiles and the responsiveness of the crew.

## Summary

- HIP toilet works start with hacking to bare brick, keeping the piping and the window frame.
- Two waterproofing layers go on the floor, followed by screed and tiling.
- The first week of hacking produces the worst dust, and the waterproofing smell lasts one to three days.
- We judged the result good value, with responsive support from the crew.
