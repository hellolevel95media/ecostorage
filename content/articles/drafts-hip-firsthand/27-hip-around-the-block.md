Most HIP guides describe the work inside your unit. The block around you changes as well. These photos come from our block during HIP, and they show the common facilities, the daily disruptions and the support available.

The picture is less dramatic than you might fear. The disruption is mostly practical, and a lot of it can be planned around.

## Before the works: the block party and showroom

Before works began, the HIP contractors hosted a block party at the void deck. There was a catered buffet, small gifts such as washcloths and towels, and a chance to meet the team and ask questions.

[[photo: common areas/block_party.jpeg | Notice for the pre-works block party at the void deck. Our flat during HIP.]]

A HIP showroom showed residents the door and gate choices, the new laundry hanging system and a mock-up of the common toilet. The mock-up was smaller than most of the toilets eventually installed, so treat showroom pieces as a guide to style, not exact dimensions.

## Common toilets at the void deck

While residents' own toilets are being upgraded, the contractors install shared toilets at the void deck. They are prefabricated shower cubicles with a shower, toilet bowl, sink, mirror and soap and shampoo dispensers.

[[photo: common areas/common_toilet_installation.jpeg | Contractors wheeling in a prefab common toilet cubicle. Our flat during HIP.]]

The resident who documented our block expected long queues, and saw one on a single Friday, with four or five people waiting, mostly domestic helpers. The working adults in our family avoided the problem by staying with relatives after the first week, for about a month. If you have somewhere else to stay, queues may never affect you.

## Temporary lifts and the laundry spot

Contractors install temporary lifts to move building materials. In our block they stood where residents normally hang laundry outdoors, so residents who were still living in their units had to dry laundry another way.

[[photo: misc/templift.jpeg | Temporary materials lift outside the block. Our flat during HIP.]]

Cordons at ground level keep foot traffic away from the lift area and other restricted spots. Part of the loading bay is also blocked off and used to store building materials in bulk.

[[photo: common areas/loading_bay_cordon.jpeg | Loading bay cordoned off and used for materials storage. Our flat during HIP.]]

## The quiet room

A quiet room is provided for residents who need a calmer place, such as adults working from home or students. It is air-conditioned and takes up most of the void deck.

[[photo: misc/quietroom.jpeg | Air-conditioned quiet room built at the void deck. Our flat during HIP.]]

It is not soundproof. We only stepped in to take that photo, and the drilling was still very audible, although vibration inside was minimal. Treat it as a cooler, cleaner place to sit, not a silent one. For how to cope at home during the works, see [Staying Home During HIP: How to Keep One Room Livable](/resources/staying-home-during-hip-one-livable-room).

## A support office you can walk into

One thing that stood out in our block was how accessible the HIP crew was. A dedicated customer support team set up an office at the ground floor, occupying part of the void deck. Residents could walk in to speak to someone, or send a WhatsApp message when the team was out surveying units.

That openness made problems easier to resolve. When we found cracked kitchen tiles, a supervisor was on the spot quickly. For what to document, see [HIP Defects and Damaged Belongings](/resources/hip-defects-damaged-belongings).

## Protecting the lifts

Contractors pad the common lifts with plastic and protective covering to prevent damage while materials and workers move through them.

[[photo: misc/commonlift.jpeg | Common lift padded with plastic and covering. Our flat during HIP.]]

If you need to move belongings in or out of the block, expect the lifts and loading areas to be busy. Plan removals for a quieter hour and check the block's notices first.

## Planning around it

- **Showers:** use the common toilets outside the morning and evening peaks, or stay elsewhere if you can.
- **Laundry:** arrange an indoor drying plan before the temporary lifts go up.
- **Questions and problems:** find the support office early, and save the team's WhatsApp number.
- **Moving items:** schedule around the lifts and the loading bay.

For whether to stay home at all, see [Move Out or Stay Home During HIP?](/resources/hip-move-out-or-stay-home). If you decide to take items out of the flat first, you can get a quote on our [storage calculator](/personal#quote).

## Frequently asked questions

### Are there common toilets during HIP?
In our block, the contractors installed prefab common toilets at the void deck. We saw one queue of four or five people on a single Friday.

### Can I hang laundry outside during HIP?
In our block, temporary lifts occupied the outdoor laundry area, so residents had to dry laundry another way.

### Is the HIP quiet room soundproof?
No. In our block, drilling was still very audible in the air-conditioned quiet room, although vibration was minimal.

### Can residents talk to the HIP team in person?
In our block, a support office at the ground floor was open to walk-ins, and the team also replied on WhatsApp.

## Summary

- Prefab common toilets at the void deck had a queue on only one Friday in our block.
- Temporary lifts can take over the usual outdoor laundry area.
- The air-conditioned quiet room is cooler but not soundproof.
- A walk-in support office and WhatsApp contact made problems quick to raise.
