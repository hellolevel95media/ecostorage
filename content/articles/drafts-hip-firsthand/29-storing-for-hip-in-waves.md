Ask us what we would change about our HIP, and the answer is rarely about tape. It is a timeline. We stored a few items, stayed in the flat, found dust on things for months and ended up exhausted. Looking back, we would run it very differently.

This article sets that plan out. It is our hindsight, not an official method, but each step answers a problem they ran into.

## What went wrong the first time

We stored the glass display shelving and some living-room furniture with EcoStorage, and kept most of the rest at home, wrapped. Dust reached everything anyway. A dining table wrapped twice in plastic ended up under a thick layer of fine dust. Silk items and a carpet could not be saved, and cleaning the flat to the point of feeling liveable took about two weeks.

The deeper problem was discovery. Items only revealed that they needed cleaning when they were moved after the works finished, so cleaning came in fragments across months. Each find, such as a thick layer behind the fridge, meant fetching the mop and bucket again. The mental strain built up. Patience ran low, tempers ran high, and it was not a good time for anyone in the family.

See [Why DIY Plastic Covering Often Fails During HIP](/resources/diy-plastic-covering-hip) for the full account of the dust.

## The plan we would use now

**1. Store nearly everything.** Fabric items such as mattresses, sofas, cushions, curtains and drapes are hard to clean. Electronics with cooling fans, speakers and desktop computers collect dust that cannot be wiped away. Fragile items and valuables belong out of the flat too. Watches and jewellery can go to a friend's or relative's home, not only to a storage company. Built-in fittings stay, but nearly everything else can leave.

[[photo: storage/_blurred/photo_3_2026-11-20_13-33-23.jpg | Wrapped shelving units and a wooden panel on a pallet in EcoStorage storage during HIP.]]

**2. Find somewhere else to stay.** The first one to two weeks are the worst, with dust that hangs in the air like a fog and constant drilling. Most adults have relatives or other short-term options. In our family, family members stayed with in-laws, partners and grandparents.

**3. Rotate the supervision.** HDB's guidance requires an adult in the unit during works hours. There were five of us who could share the job, so we would plan a daily rota, with each person taking leave or an off day in turn, so nobody carries the whole burden. For the reasons the supervisor matters, see [Strangers Walking Into Your Flat During HIP](/resources/strangers-walking-into-flat-during-hip).

**4. Send things in waves.** You do not need to empty the flat in a day. Send items you rarely use first, then things you use occasionally about a week before, and the essentials, such as the sofa or the dining table, in the last day or two. Your comfort tapers down to nothing just as the works begin.

[[photo: storage/_blurred/photo_2_2026-11-20_13-33-23.jpg | A wrapped cabinet on a tagged pallet in EcoStorage storage during HIP.]]

**5. Bring things back three days before the projected finish.** We noticed that near the end of the schedule, mostly contractors came in and out for inspections. There was no more drilling or hacking to kick up dust. That is the moment to start cleaning.

## Turn the end into a spring clean

Bringing items back early has a bonus. While the stored pieces come out of their wrapping, you can give each one a wipe down. At the same time, you can clean the built-in furniture that stayed in the flat. With floors bare and little to move, a final mop and wipe of the whole unit takes one to two hours.

It would have been a fantastic opportunity for a spring clean. We missed it because we had not planned and forecast the timeline. A single planned cleanup replaces months of surprises.

## Practical steps

- **Make the list room by room,** and mark each item as store, relative's home or stays.
- **Pick your wave dates** against the HIP schedule. For what happens each day, see [A Real 10-Day HIP Unit Schedule](/resources/real-10-day-hip-unit-schedule).
- **Book storage and collection early,** since slots fill and you will want the return date fixed.
- **Set the supervision rota** before the first day, and share it with everyone who will take a turn.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I store during HIP?
Fabric items, electronics with fans, fragile pieces, curtains and drapes, and valuables. Built-in fittings cannot be removed, but nearly everything else can.

### When should I bring stored items back?
We would bring them back about three days before the projected completion, when mostly inspectors are coming in and no more drilling or hacking is under way.

### How can a family share the supervision?
By setting a rota in advance, so each adult takes leave or an off day in turn. We had five people to rotate between.

### Do valuables have to go into a storage service?
No. A friend's or a relative's home works for watches, jewellery and similar items.

## Summary

- We stored little, cleaned for months and would now store nearly everything.
- Send items in waves, and keep what you need until the last day or two.
- Rotate supervision across family members, and stay elsewhere if you can.
- Bring items back about three days before the end, and clean everything once.
