Many people assume they can keep working from home during HIP with a good pair of headphones. When one of us tried it in our flat, we found the noise manageable and the vibration very hard to ignore.

This article describes that experience, the limits of the quiet room, and a few ways to plan around it.

## Noise is solvable, up to a point

Drilling and hacking are loud. With a good wireless headset, a person can overcome most of the sound for calls and focused work. If sound were the only problem, working at home would be tiring but possible.

## Vibration is not

Headphones do nothing about vibration. When a neighbouring unit is being drilled, the vibration comes through the walls and floor, and it affects daily tasks in ways that surprised we:

- **Writing on paper becomes difficult,** because the surface under your hand keeps shaking.
- **Things near table edges can fall off.** A cup, a phone or a stack of papers placed close to the edge can move and drop when the unit next door is being drilled.
- **It is physically tiring,** which wears down your concentration through the day.

Our advice is to keep a clear zone around your desk, avoid placing anything fragile near edges, and put key items on a stable surface.

## The sound barrier and the quiet room

Contractors hang a sound-barrier sheet during the most intensive hacking and drilling. In our flat, it helped very little, because most of the sound travels through the walls. It looked more like a visual cue than a practical shield.

The block also provides a quiet room at the void deck for people who need a calmer place, such as adults working from home or students. It is air-conditioned. We member who visited it, only for a photo, found that the drilling was still very audible, although vibration inside was minimal.

[[photo: misc/quietroom.jpeg | Air-conditioned quiet room built at the void deck. Our flat during HIP.]]

If you need to work in the quiet room, the lower vibration may be worth it, even if the noise is not fully blocked. Bring headphones. For more on the facilities in the block, see [Life Around the Block During HIP](/resources/life-around-the-block-during-hip).

## Why it lasts for months

The ten-day schedule covers your own unit. HIP proceeds in a rolling pattern across the block, and when one unit is finished and waiting for inspection, the crew moves to another. Drilling continues until the whole block is done. In our block, that took months. Even after your own works ended, the noise and vibration from neighbours' units carried on.

For the schedule itself, see [A Real 10-Day HIP Unit Schedule](/resources/real-10-day-hip-unit-schedule).

## What helps

- **Work somewhere else for the worst weeks.** The first one to two weeks of your unit's works are the hardest. A relative's home or a co-working space may be much easier.
- **Ask your employer in advance.** If you need flexibility, raise it before the works begin.
- **Use headphones and a stable desk.** Place the desk away from the walls that border drilling units, and keep objects away from the edges.
- **Remember the dust.** Dust affects computers and equipment, so electronics are best stored or covered. See [Protecting TVs, Computers and Electronics During HIP Works](/resources/protect-electronics-during-hip).

If you plan to move your desk setup out of the flat, you can get a quote on our [storage calculator](/personal#quote). In our flat, the glass display shelving and some living-room furniture went into EcoStorage storage for the HIP period.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I work from home during HIP?
It is possible, but for us vibration made it very uncomfortable, even with a wireless headset that handled the noise.

### Does vibration really affect desk work?
Yes. Writing on paper became hard, and items near table edges could fall off when the next-door unit was being drilled.

### Does the HIP quiet room block the noise?
No. The drilling was still very audible in the quiet room, though vibration was minimal.

### How long does HIP drilling last?
Your own unit takes about ten working days, but the block's rolling schedule means drilling continues until every unit is finished, which took months in our block.

## Summary

- Wireless headsets can handle HIP noise, but they do nothing about vibration.
- Vibration made writing hard and moved items near table edges.
- The quiet room has less vibration but is not soundproof.
- Drilling continues for months across the block, so plan to work elsewhere for the worst weeks.
