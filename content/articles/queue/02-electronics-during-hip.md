Electronics are often the most expensive items in a flat and the least tolerant of renovation dust. The fine particles from hacking, cement mixing and screeding get into vents, ports and cooling fans. The damage rarely shows straight away. It appears weeks later as a computer that runs hot, a TV that will not start or a router that keeps dropping out.

This guide explains why dust is so harmful, how to prepare and pack each kind of device, and how to switch things back on safely when the works finish.

## Why dust and electronics don't mix

Renovation dust is much finer than normal household dust, and a lot of it is made of abrasive mineral particles. Inside a device it causes three problems:

- It clogs fans and vents, so the device cannot cool itself and overheats
- It settles on circuit boards, where it can absorb moisture from Singapore's humid air and create small electrical faults
- It gets into ports, buttons and disc drives, causing poor connections and wear

Plastic sheeting taped around a device does not solve the problem. A device that is left plugged in under a cover can overheat. And if the cover is not sealed, dust still gets in through the gaps.

## Treat electronics as the first category to move

Move electronics out before anything else, not after. The ideal is to take them out of the unit entirely, or at least into a room that has no works at all. A sealed box in a room being hacked is still exposed to dust and knocks.

## Before you pack: a preparation checklist

A little preparation saves a lot of trouble later:

- Back up computers, phones, tablets and external drives. If something goes wrong, you still have your data
- Power every device completely down, and unplug it
- Photograph the cable layout behind the TV, the router and the computer, so setting up again takes minutes rather than an hour
- Label cables with masking tape (for example "TV power", "router to modem")
- Keep remotes, adapters and small cables together in one bag, labelled with the device name
- Remove batteries from remotes and accessories to avoid leaks
- Remove discs from drives, and take printer ink cartridges out if the printer will be stored for weeks

## How to pack each type of device

## Televisions

Original boxes are best, because the foam is shaped to the TV. If you no longer have it, wrap the screen in a soft clean cloth, then bubble wrap, and use flat cardboard on both faces. Large TVs travel safest upright rather than flat, with the screen protected on both sides. Never place heavy items on top of a TV or lean anything against the screen.

## Desktop computers and monitors

Wipe the outside of the case with a dry cloth. Pack the tower upright in a box with padding on every side. Monitors need the same care as TVs: cloth, bubble wrap and cardboard faces. Keep the stand and its screws together in a labelled bag.

## Laptops, tablets and consoles

These are small enough to carry yourself, and often the best choice. Store them in a closed drawer or box in a room with no works, wrapped in a clean cloth, with a silica gel packet if possible. Game consoles should be packed with their cables and controllers in a padded box.

## Routers, modems and set-top boxes

Many households leave these running through the works to keep internet access, and then find them clogged with dust. If you need connectivity, put the router in a room that is sealed off and has no works, away from walls that are being hacked. Otherwise, pack it and set it up again after cleaning.

## Speakers, printers and small appliances

Wrap speaker cones gently with a soft cloth, and avoid pressing on them. Printers should be cleaned of paper and ink, and covered with a cloth or a box. Small appliances such as air fryers, kettles and rice cookers belong in boxes, wiped clean and fully dry.

## Humidity matters as much as dust

Singapore's humidity is hard on electronics left sealed for weeks. Put a few silica gel packets in each box, and avoid sealing devices in airtight plastic bags. A breathable cardboard box with moisture absorbers works better than a sealed bag. Do not store electronics directly on the floor, where moisture and any water from cleaning can reach them.

## Where to put them

If you have a spare room with no works, a tall cupboard that can be shut is usually safe. If you do not, taking electronics to storage is the safest choice. ## Switching things back on after the works

Do not plug everything in immediately.

- Wait until the unit has been cleaned and the dust has settled
- Unpack devices in a clean room and let them sit for an hour or two to reach room temperature, because a cold device in warm humid air can form condensation inside
- Check vents and ports for visible dust, and clean them gently with a dry soft brush or a can of compressed air, holding fans still while you do so
- Reconnect cables using your photos and labels
- Power on and watch for unusual noise, heat or smells in the first few minutes. If anything seems wrong, turn it off and have it checked

## A quick checklist

- Back up and power down
- Photograph and label cables
- Pack in original boxes where possible, with moisture absorbers
- Move out of rooms with works
- Clean vents before switching on

A little planning here protects some of the most expensive things you own, and costs far less than a repair or a replacement.
