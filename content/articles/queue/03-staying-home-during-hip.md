Many residents stay in their flat through HIP rather than move out. That is workable, but only if you plan for one room that stays genuinely clean and usable while the rest of the unit is worked on. Without a plan, that room quickly becomes a storeroom, and the whole household ends up living in a corner of clutter and dust.

This guide covers how to choose and seal a base room, what daily life looks like during the works, and how to clear out the rest of the flat so the base room stays usable.

## Choose your base room first

Pick the room before the works start. The best choice usually has these features:

- It is as far as possible from the toilet and kitchen works, which tend to be the dustiest
- It has a door that closes well, with a small gap underneath
- It has a window or ventilation you can control
- It has a power point near where you will sleep or work
- It is big enough for a bed or mattress, a small table and a few boxes

Treat this room as your base for the whole period: sleeping, working, charging devices and keeping essentials such as medicine, documents and a few days of clothes.

## How to seal the room

Dust finds every gap, so sealing the room well pays off:

- Tape the edges of the door with painter's tape, or use a draught strip along the bottom
- Cover vents and the aircon outlet with plastic and tape if you are not using them
- Put a damp towel along the bottom of the door during the dustiest days
- Keep the door shut during working hours, and use it as little as you can
- Put a doormat or a damp cloth just outside, and take shoes off before you go in

If you rely on aircon in the base room, check with your contractor whether it is safe to run it during the works and whether the filters need covering or cleaning afterwards.

## Set up a "dust zone"

Create a small area just outside your base room, or at the front door, where you take off shoes and any dusty outer clothing before you go in. A simple hook for jackets and a bin for dust masks keeps the room itself cleaner. Wipe your phone and keys with a cloth when you come in, as they pick up dust surprisingly often.

## The base room fills up fast

The catch is that this room is usually also where everything else ends up. Furniture from the rest of the flat gets pushed in, and the "clean room" becomes a storeroom. The fix is to move things out of the unit entirely, rather than just into the next room.

Decide the minimum you need day to day:

- A bed or mattress
- A small table and a chair
- A small wardrobe, rack or a couple of boxes for clothes
- A fan or small appliance if needed

Everything else can wait in storage until the works are finished.

## Daily life during the works

A few practical points make the weeks easier.

## Meals

Cooking will be limited, or impossible, while kitchen works are underway. Plan simple meals, use a small induction cooker or kettle in the base room if the power load allows, and keep a small supply of water, snacks and cutlery there. Plan for takeaway or meals out on the dustiest days.

## Laundry and bathing

HDB sets up facilities for residents during works, as described in our HIP timeline guide. Keep a laundry bag in the base room and a separate basket for dusty clothes, so dust does not get onto clean ones.

## Working from home

Noise is the main problem for anyone working from home. Hacking and drilling can be very loud, so plan to work elsewhere on the noisiest days, or book a co-working space, a library or a family member's home. Keep headphones, chargers and a small amount of paperwork in the base room.

## Children, older family members and pets

Think carefully about who is at home during working hours. Young children, older family members and anyone with asthma or breathing sensitivities may be better away from the unit on the dustiest days. If someone has a medical condition, speak to their doctor about masks and about staying elsewhere for the worst days. Pets can be stressed by noise and may need to stay with friends or family.

## Protecting what stays

Large furniture that cannot leave the unit still needs protection. Cover it with sheeting, and tape the sheeting to the floor so dust cannot creep under it. Our guide on bedroom furniture covers beds, wardrobes and mattresses in detail, and our other articles on HIP preparation explain how to protect sofas, cabinets and electronics.

## Clearing the rest of the flat

Clearing the rest of the flat is the biggest job. Bulky pieces such as sofas, dining tables, bookshelves and extra beds are the ones that take over your base room. A simple approach:

- Walk through every room and tag each large item as "stays", "covers" or "goes out"
- Move anything you do not need during the works into storage
- Cover the items that remain, and take photos for reference
- Bring the "goes out" items back in the reverse order, starting with what you need first

Moving large pieces to storage until the works are complete, then back again, gives you the room back. Our team can collect and return them on dates that suit your schedule.

## After the works

Do the cleaning in the right order:

- Leave the base room until last, then move out of it into the cleaned areas
- Clean from top to bottom: ceiling, walls, shelves, then floors
- Wipe surfaces, vacuum fabric and wash curtains and covers
- Bring furniture and boxes back once the floors are dry

Setting up like this takes a weekend, but it makes the weeks of works far more tolerable, particularly for families with children or older members.
