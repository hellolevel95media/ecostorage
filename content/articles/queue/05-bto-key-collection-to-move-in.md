Key collection feels like the finish line, but for most BTO households it is the start of a longer middle stretch. Renovation, inspections, defect checks and furniture delivery all come before you can actually live in the flat.

During that stretch you are often paying for two places: the old home you still occupy or rent, and the new one you cannot use yet. Where your belongings sit during this time affects both your costs and your stress. This guide walks through the sequence and explains how to avoid handling everything twice.

## The typical sequence

Every household is different, but a common order looks like this:

- Collect the keys
- Inspect the unit and note any defects
- Appoint a contractor and plan the renovation
- Carry out the renovation
- Clean the unit after the works
- Deliver furniture and appliances
- Move in

Furniture and appliances ideally arrive near the end, once the dust is gone. Delivering a sofa into a unit full of workers risks damage and wastes the effort.

## Inspect the unit properly

When you first collect the keys, take time to check the flat carefully. Look at floors, walls, ceilings, doors, windows, switches, taps and the bathroom. Take photos and note anything that looks wrong. There is an official process for reporting defects after key collection, so check HDB's current guidance for the steps and deadlines, and follow them as early as you can.

Measure each room at the same time. Accurate measurements of rooms, doors and the entrance help you decide which furniture will fit, and guide your carpenter and contractor.

## The gap you have to plan for

Between key collection and move-in, your belongings need somewhere to be. A few common approaches:

- Keep everything at your current home until the renovation finishes, then move once
- Move non-essentials out early to free up space and reduce clutter
- Store furniture and boxes that will go straight into the new unit, and have them delivered on a set date after the works

The third option is popular because it cuts one stage out of the process. Items go from your old place to storage, and from storage directly into the finished flat, with no double handling and no pile of boxes sitting in a room being renovated.

## Why not simply move in early?

It is tempting to move boxes into the new flat as soon as you have the keys. The problems are:

- Dust and paint get onto boxes and furniture
- Items are in the way of the contractors, who may need to move them repeatedly
- Boxes can be damaged or go missing in a busy worksite
- You risk delays if works overrun and the stored items are in the way

Keeping things in storage until the unit is ready avoids all of these.

## Decide what to bring

A new home is a good moment to be selective. Before you move anything:

- Measure the new rooms and compare them with your existing furniture
- Decide which pieces will not fit the new layout
- Sell or donate items you will not use
- Think about whether older furniture is worth moving twice

Moving something that does not fit is a waste of both time and money.

## Pack by destination, not by origin

When you pack, label boxes by the room they will go to in the new flat, not by where they came from. For example, "Master bedroom: clothes" or "Kitchen: pots and pans". On delivery day, the team places each box in the right room, and you start unpacking straight away.

Add a number to each box and keep a simple list of what each number contains. It makes it easy to find what you need first.

## Plan the delivery date around sign-off

Choose your delivery date around the contractor's final sign-off and cleaning, not around the original end date. If the works overrun, flexible storage lets you hold things a little longer without scrambling. A cleaning service after renovation also helps, because even a well-managed renovation leaves dust that needs to be gone before furniture goes in.

## Protect your new floors and walls

On delivery day, a few simple steps protect your new home:

- Lay protective sheets or boards on floors along the route
- Cover door frames and corners where large items will pass
- Measure large items against doors and lifts before delivery
- Keep a clear path from the lift to the unit

## Items to keep with you

Some items should travel with you rather than going into storage or onto a truck:

- Documents, such as keys, agreements, receipts and warranties
- Valuables and jewellery
- Medicine and daily essentials
- A small box with tools, cleaning supplies and lightbulbs for the first night

## A simple timeline

- Four to six weeks before delivery: confirm dates, finish the inventory and book storage collection
- Two weeks before: confirm the renovation finish date and book cleaning
- One week before: check access arrangements, lift booking and parking at the new block
- Delivery day: be present, walk through each room, and tick off boxes against your list

## Final thought

Treating the move as two distinct stages, renovation first and delivery second, makes the whole process calmer.
