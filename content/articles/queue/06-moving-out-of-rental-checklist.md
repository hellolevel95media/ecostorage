At the end of a tenancy, most disputes come down to the same things: marks on walls, general cleanliness, and anything that was damaged or missing. A little planning protects your deposit and shortens the argument. This guide walks through the process, from reading your agreement to handing back the keys.

This article is general guidance, not legal advice. Always follow your own tenancy agreement, and if you are unsure about your rights, ask your agent or a professional.

## Start with your tenancy agreement

Read it again, properly, before you start. Look for:

- Cleaning requirements, including whether professional cleaning is needed
- Rules on repainting, repairs and wear and tear
- Aircon servicing requirements and how often
- Notice periods for ending the lease
- Whether you must restore the unit to its original condition
- Any inventory list attached to the agreement

Knowing what you are responsible for tells you what to do, and what to ignore.

## Four weeks to go: plan

Work backwards from the move-out date:

- Confirm the end date and notice requirements in writing
- Book movers, cleaners and any aircon servicing early, because the end of the month is busy
- Decide where your belongings will go, including any gap before the next home is ready
- Start decluttering, so there is less to move

## Two weeks to go: document

Take photos and videos of every room as it is now, including walls, floors, appliances, cabinets, bathroom fittings and the balcony. Date-stamped photos are best. If there is an inventory list from move-in, compare it with what is there now. Note anything that was already marked or damaged before you moved in, and send the photos to the landlord or agent if you did not do so at the start.

## Pack and clear

Remove all personal items, including from cabinets, drawers, balconies, storerooms and the area above wardrobes. Leave nothing behind, including small items such as nails, hooks and adhesive strips.

## A room-by-room checklist

## Living and bedrooms

- Patch small nail holes if your agreement requires it, and keep receipts for materials
- Wipe skirting boards, doors, switches and window frames
- Vacuum or mop floors, and clean under furniture that stays
- Wash or wipe curtains and blinds if they are yours or if the landlord expects it
- Check wardrobes and shelves are empty and clean

## Kitchen

- Clean the hob, oven and extractor hood, which often attract the most comments
- Defrost and clean the fridge if it belongs to the unit, and leave the door ajar
- Clean the sink, taps and cabinets inside and out
- Clean the microwave, if provided

## Bathrooms

- Scrub tiles, grout, the toilet, basin and shower area
- Descale taps and showerheads
- Remove any items from drawers and the cabinet
- Check drains are clear

## Balcony and utility areas

- Sweep and wash the floor
- Clean the washing line or rack, the drain and the windows
- Check that the washing machine area is clean and dry

## Aircon and appliances

Many agreements require the aircon to be serviced before you leave, with a receipt as proof. Book the service well ahead. Keep manuals, remotes and any spare parts together, and leave them in a visible spot for the landlord.

## Cleaning: do it yourself or hire?

If your agreement requires professional cleaning, get a receipt. If it does not, a thorough clean yourself usually works, but it takes time. Be realistic about how long it takes, especially if you are also moving the same day.

## Moving day itself

Empty the unit completely before the final inspection. Furniture piled in the middle of a room hides marks and makes the handover harder. A clear, clean unit with photos to back it up is the strongest position to be in.

Take final photos after the unit is empty and cleaned. These are the ones that matter most.

## Keys, access cards and meter readings

Hand over every set of keys and access cards, including those for the gate, letterbox and any carpark. Take photos of utility meter readings, and close or transfer your utility accounts. Ask the landlord or agent to confirm the handover in writing, even by message.

## Keep your records

Keep photos, messages, receipts and the signed handover note until your deposit has been returned. If there is a disagreement, these are the best evidence you can have.
