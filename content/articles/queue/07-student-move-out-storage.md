When a semester ends, students living in halls, hostels or shared rooms often have to clear out completely, sometimes within a few days. If you are heading home or overseas for the break, there is nowhere obvious to leave a mini fridge, a bike, a desk lamp and several boxes of books.

Carrying it all home is costly if home is far away, and not everyone has space waiting for them. Asking a friend to keep it works for a box or two, but not for a whole room. This guide helps students and parents plan the move-out, sort what to keep, and pack so everything comes back in good shape.

## Start early and sort

Begin a couple of weeks before the move-out date. Put everything you own into three piles:

- Take with you: what you need over the break or will use at your next accommodation
- Store: what you will want next semester
- Sell, give away or recycle: what you no longer need

Students tend to underestimate how much they have accumulated, and the sorting alone cuts the load. Be honest about things you have not used all semester.

## Check your hall's move-out rules

Many halls and hostels have strict clearing dates, limited time slots for loading and rules about what can be left behind. Check these early, and book storage collection around them rather than relying on the last day. Some halls have limited lift access or loading bays at set times, so ask about this in advance.

## What to store and what not to

Good candidates for storage:

- Books, notes and stationery
- Small appliances, such as a mini fridge, fan, kettle or rice cooker
- Bedding, pillows and a lightweight mattress topper
- Desk lamp, organisers and decorations
- Seasonal clothes
- Bicycles, if the storage provider accepts them (check first)

Items to take with you or leave with someone you trust:

- Passports, IDs, bank cards and other documents
- Laptops, phones and valuables
- Medication
- Anything perishable

## How to pack

## Boxes and weights

Use sturdy boxes of a manageable size. Books are heavy, so use small boxes and fill them completely, with paper in any gaps. Clothes and bedding can go in larger boxes, which are lighter. If you are straining, the box is too heavy.

## Labelling

Write on the side of each box, not the top, because boxes get stacked. Include your name, a short description of the contents and a number. Keep a simple list on your phone with a photo of each box, so you can find what you need on return.

## Clothes and bedding

Wash and fully dry everything before packing. Damp fabric in a closed box is a mould risk, and Singapore's humidity makes it worse. Use cardboard boxes or breathable bags, rather than sealed plastic. Put a silica gel packet in each box if you can.

## Preparing small appliances

Small appliances need a little care:

- Defrost and dry mini fridges completely, wipe the inside, and leave the door ajar during storage
- Clean rice cookers, kettles and microwaves, and make sure they are completely dry
- Wrap cables and keep them with the matching device
- Take photos of the setup if it has several parts

## Bicycles, scooters and bulky items

Ask the provider whether they accept bicycles and e-scooters. Batteries need special handling, and some providers do not accept them at all. Clean the frame, lower the seat, and protect it with a cover or a blanket.

## Protect books and paper

Books absorb moisture. Stack them flat in small boxes, put a sheet of paper between layers if they are valuable, and avoid damp areas. A silica gel packet in the box helps.

## Think about the cost

A few boxes and a small appliance fit into a compact space, so a per-module price makes more sense than a large unit. Compare costs for short periods, and ask whether there are minimum commitments. If you are returning for the next semester, flexible or month-to-month arrangements let you retrieve things on your own schedule with a few days' notice.

## For parents arranging this

Parents often organise storage on behalf of students. A few things make it smoother:

- Agree on who will be at the hall on collection day
- Share the inventory and photos so everyone knows what is going
- Make sure there is a clear way to pay, and a contact who can authorise delivery
- Confirm dates for return well before the next semester starts

## For students going overseas

If you are going home for a long break, or your plans might change, think about who can collect or accept your belongings if you are not in Singapore. Nominate someone and share the details in advance.

## A last-week checklist

- Finish sorting and packing at least two days before move-out
- Photograph every box and keep the list
- Take your documents and valuables with you
- Confirm collection time with the storage provider and the hall
- Do a final walk-through of your room, including inside wardrobes and under the bed

Planning a week or two ahead turns a stressful move-out into a simple, organised process.
