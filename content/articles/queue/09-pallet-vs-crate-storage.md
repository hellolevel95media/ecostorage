When people picture storage, they imagine a locked room with a roller door. At EcoStorage, belongings are stored in one of two ways, and knowing the difference helps you choose the right one for what you own, and avoid paying for protection you do not need.

This guide explains how pallet and crate storage work, what suits each, and how to pack for both.

## How pallet storage works

Pallet storage is the standard option. Your items are placed on recycled wooden pallets and bundled together with industrial shrink wrap. This does three useful things:

- Everything is lifted off the floor, so nothing touches the warehouse floor
- Your belongings are kept separate from other customers' items
- The wrapped bundle moves as one unit, so handling is efficient and items are less likely to shift

It suits most household goods: boxes, furniture, appliances, bags and general items. Because pallets are handled with equipment designed for them, large items are moved with care rather than carried by hand.

## How crate storage works

Crate storage is the premium upgrade. Your items go inside a fully enclosed wooden crate with swinging doors. The crate adds a rigid outer layer, which protects against knocks and light, and it limits contact with air and dust.

Crates make sense for items that are more sensitive to handling, light or conditions, or simply too valuable to risk.

## Which suits which item?

A rough guide:

- Pallet: everyday furniture, boxed clothes and books, appliances, kitchenware, sports gear, seasonal decorations, general household goods
- Crate: musical instruments, artwork, framed pictures, wine, antiques, leather goods, electronics, collectibles and anything you would hate to see damaged

It is not only about value. Think about what each item tolerates:

- Solid wood furniture copes well with ambient conditions
- Leather, photographs, instruments and veneered furniture are more sensitive, and do better in an enclosed crate
- Electronics benefit from the extra shielding from dust and knocks
- Mattresses and upholstered pieces need good covers whichever method you choose

## Ambient storage and what it means for you

Most of our warehouse space runs at ambient temperature rather than being air-conditioned around the clock. This keeps energy use low, which is part of our environmental approach. A small share of our space is climate-controlled, and it is available as an option for items that need it.

Whichever method you choose, good packing matters, particularly in Singapore's humid climate:

- Make sure everything is completely dry before it goes in
- Use breathable covers for fabric and upholstered items, rather than sealed plastic
- Put moisture absorbers, such as silica gel, in boxes
- Leave small gaps between items where you can, so air can move

## Packing for pallet storage

- Use sturdy boxes of similar sizes, which stack and wrap more neatly
- Put heavier boxes at the bottom and lighter ones on top
- Label every box on the side, with contents and a number
- Wrap furniture with moving blankets or padding before it is bundled
- Take photos of items before they are wrapped, and keep an inventory

## Packing for crate storage

- Wrap each item individually in soft padding, then place it in the crate
- Keep items from touching each other, using padding between them
- Stand items upright where appropriate, such as paintings and instruments
- Add moisture absorbers
- Photograph the crate contents before closing

## Can you mix both?

Yes, and many customers do. A typical arrangement is to put furniture and boxes on pallets, and a few special pieces in a crate. That keeps the cost sensible while protecting what matters most. You can also upgrade a particular item to a crate later, rather than moving everything.

## Cost and practical differences

Crate storage costs more than pallet storage because the crate itself and its space add to the price. For most households, standard pallets are all that is needed. A useful approach is to look at your inventory and ask which items would be expensive or impossible to replace, then protect those.

## Retrieval

For both methods, you request items back, and we schedule delivery on a date that suits you. A few days' notice helps us plan properly. If you need only some of your items, tell us in advance and keep an inventory so we can find them quickly.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Which items would I be most upset to lose or damage?
- Which are sensitive to light, humidity or handling?
- How long will they be in storage?
- Do I need to access any of them during the storage period?

## How to decide

If you are unsure, tell us what you are storing and for how long. We will suggest the option that fits, and you can upgrade a particular item to a crate rather than moving everything. Our calculator shows the price difference before you commit.
