Singapore is warm and humid all year, and that is exactly the environment mould likes. Items can come out of storage smelling musty even when nothing has leaked, simply because they went in slightly damp or were sealed in the wrong way.

The good news is that most mould problems start before storage, and you can prevent them with habits that cost very little. This guide explains how mould forms, how to prepare different kinds of items, and what to do if you find a problem.

## How mould forms

Mould needs three things: moisture, a food source and time. Almost everything in your home provides food, including fabric, paper, leather, wood and even dust. Humid air provides moisture. If an item goes into storage slightly damp, or is sealed in a way that traps humidity, mould can begin to appear in days to weeks.

You can control two of these factors. Make sure items are completely dry and clean, which removes moisture and food, and pack them so air can circulate.

## The golden rule: completely dry

Everything must be completely dry when it goes in. That includes items that look dry but have been in contact with moisture:

- Wash and fully dry clothes, bedding, curtains and soft furnishings. Check seams and pockets, where moisture lingers
- Wipe down furniture and leave it to air for a day or two
- Dry bathroom items, such as mats and towels, thoroughly
- Let items that were recently cleaned dry completely before wrapping

Even slightly damp wood or fabric can develop spots within weeks once it is closed in.

## Clean before storing

Dirt and body oils feed mould. Wash clothes and linen, wipe leather and furniture, and vacuum upholstered items. Do not store anything with food residue, such as lunch boxes or kitchenware.

## Choose the right wrapping

Sealed plastic bags look protective but can trap moisture against fabric. Breathable options work better:

- Cotton sheets or breathable furniture covers for sofas, chairs and mattresses
- Cardboard boxes lined with clean paper for clothes, rather than airtight bags
- Wardrobe-style boxes or hanging covers for formal wear
- Acid-free tissue paper for delicate textiles

Avoid newspaper, which can transfer ink onto fabric.

## Add a moisture absorber

Silica gel packets or moisture-absorbing containers placed in boxes and wardrobes help control humidity. Place them where air collects, such as on top of items in a box. Replace or recharge them if you store for several months.

## Keep items off the ground

In our facilities, boxes and furniture sit on pallets so nothing touches the floor. If you store anything yourself, put a barrier between the item and the ground. Moisture can seep up from concrete and tiles.

## Leave space for air

Tightly packed boxes with no airflow hold humidity longer. Where possible:

- Leave small gaps between boxes
- Leave drawers slightly open or emptied
- Do not overfill boxes, so air can reach the contents

## Material-by-material guide

## Fabric, clothes and bedding

Wash, dry completely and store in breathable boxes with moisture absorbers. Avoid vacuum bags for long periods, which can crush fibres and hold humidity.

## Leather bags, shoes and furniture

Leather is vulnerable to mould and drying out. Clean it with a leather-appropriate cloth, let it dry fully, stuff bags with acid-free paper to hold their shape, and store them in a breathable dust bag. Avoid plastic.

## Wooden furniture

Wipe clean and dry. Wood copes well with ambient conditions, but unfinished wood is more vulnerable. Use breathable covers or blankets, and avoid direct contact with the floor.

## Books and paper

Stack flat in small boxes, avoid overfilling and put a moisture absorber in each box. Keep photographs and documents in acid-free sleeves or folders.

## Mattresses and upholstered furniture

Vacuum and spot-clean, then let them dry completely. Use breathable covers.

## Electronics

Pack in original boxes where possible, with silica gel, and avoid sealing in airtight plastic.

## Musical instruments, artwork and collectibles

These benefit from enclosed crate storage or, for a small share of our space, a climate-controlled area. Ask us what suits your items.

## What to check when items come back

When your belongings return, unpack and air them promptly instead of leaving them sealed. In the first few days:

- Open boxes and let them breathe
- Check for musty smells, spots or discolouration
- Look inside drawers and cupboards
- Shake out fabrics and hang them in a ventilated area

Tell us straight away if you notice anything unexpected, so we can look into it.

## If you find mould

Small spots on hard surfaces can usually be wiped with a damp cloth and mild detergent, then dried thoroughly. Fabrics that can be washed should be washed on the hottest setting their label allows, then dried completely. Wear a mask and gloves when handling mouldy items, and keep them away from other things. If mould is widespread, or on leather, antiques or valuable items, consult a professional cleaner or restorer. Discard anything that cannot be cleaned and could affect health.

## A quick checklist

- Clean and completely dry everything
- Use breathable covers and boxes
- Add moisture absorbers
- Keep items off the floor and leave a little air space
- Unpack and inspect promptly on return

A little preparation goes a long way, and it is far easier than trying to remove mould afterwards.
