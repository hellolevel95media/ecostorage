Large appliances are often the heaviest part of a move, and the easiest to damage if they are not prepared properly. A few steps before collection protect both the appliance and the items stored beside it. This guide covers the common appliances one by one, plus general tips on wrapping, moving and photographing them.

## General rules for every appliance

Before you start on any specific appliance:

- Unplug it and let it cool completely if it has been in use
- Clean it inside and out, and make sure it is completely dry
- Remove loose parts, such as trays, racks and filters, and pack them separately
- Wrap cables and keep them with the appliance
- Keep manuals, warranty cards and remote controls together
- Take photos of the appliance, its serial number and any existing marks

## Fridges and freezers

Fridges are the most common problem, because a closed, damp fridge develops mould and odours very quickly.

- Empty it completely, including the freezer, the door shelves and the drawers
- Switch off and unplug at least a day before collection, preferably two for a large freezer
- Defrost the freezer, using towels to catch water, and never chip the ice with a sharp tool
- Wipe the inside with a mild solution of baking soda and water, then dry thoroughly
- Remove shelves, drawers and trays, wash and dry them, and wrap them separately
- Leave the door slightly open, held with a soft stopper or a rolled towel, so air circulates
- Tape or secure the door for transport, using painter's tape on the outside

Fridges should travel upright whenever possible. If a fridge has been laid on its side, let it stand upright for several hours before switching it on, so the oil in the compressor can settle.

## Washing machines

Washing machines need draining so water does not sit inside.

- Turn off the water supply and disconnect the inlet hose
- Run a short drain cycle, and drain any remaining water from the hose and filter
- Clean the detergent drawer and the rubber seal, and wipe them dry
- Leave the door ajar so the drum can dry
- Secure the drum if your model has transit bolts, or use tape on the door and the cable so nothing swings loose
- Wrap hoses together and keep them with the machine

## Dryers

Clean the lint filter and the lint trap, wipe the drum, and make sure it is dry. Leave the door slightly ajar. Wrap the cable and any exhaust hose.

## Dishwashers

Run a short drain cycle, remove the racks and filter, and clean them. Dry the inside and leave the door ajar. Secure hoses and the cable.

## Ovens, hobs and microwaves

Clean thoroughly, including grease on racks and trays. Remove the turntable from the microwave, wrap it, and secure the door with tape. For built-in ovens, only a professional should disconnect them.

## Air-conditioners

Have the unit professionally removed, because the refrigerant and electrical connections need proper handling. Store the indoor and outdoor parts separately, keep caps on the pipes, and wrap the remote and any brackets with each unit.

## Water dispensers, kettles and rice cookers

Empty them, descale if needed, and dry completely. Leave lids open or ajar. Wrap cords.

## Vacuum cleaners and small appliances

Empty the dust bag or bin, clean filters, and wrap cords. Pack attachments in a labelled bag.

## TVs and electronics

Pack in the original box if you have it. Otherwise wrap the screen in a soft cloth and bubble wrap, and keep it upright. Our guide on protecting electronics covers this in more detail.

## Fans and heaters

Clean the blades and grille, wrap them in a breathable cover and leave them upright. Remove batteries from remote controls.

## Wrapping sensibly

Wrap the surface with moving blankets or breathable covers to protect against scratches, rather than sealing the appliance tightly in plastic for long storage, which can trap moisture. Use cardboard on corners and edges, and keep doors closed or taped.

## Moving heavy items safely

Heavy appliances should be carried by trained movers with the right equipment, particularly down stairs or through narrow doors. Do not try to move a fridge or washing machine alone. Mention anything that is hard to reach when you ask for a quote, such as stairs, narrow corridors or long distances from the lift to the truck, so extras are discussed in advance rather than on the day.

## Document before you move

Take a photo of each appliance, its serial number and any scratches. Keep the manual and warranty with your inventory. This helps if there is any question about condition later.

## Is it worth storing?

If you are not sure whether an old appliance is worth storing, compare the storage cost over several months with the price of a replacement. A fridge that is ten years old and costs more to store than to replace may be better sold or recycled. A newer, high-quality appliance is usually worth keeping.

## When your appliances come back

Let fridges and freezers stand for a few hours before plugging them in. Check hoses and connections on washing machines, and run an empty cycle before the first load. Clean any dust from vents. If something does not work as expected, tell us promptly so we can help.
