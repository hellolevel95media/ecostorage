Both options give you somewhere to keep your belongings, but they work very differently. Choosing well depends on how you live, not just on price. This article compares them honestly, including where self-storage is the better choice.

## How self-storage works

With traditional self-storage, you rent a unit, usually a small room with a lockable door. You are responsible for:

- Packing your belongings
- Arranging transport, such as a van, a car or a mover
- Carrying everything in, and out again
- Visiting the facility whenever you need something

Many facilities run air-conditioning around the clock, and offer access during set hours. The benefit is control: you decide when you visit and how items are arranged.

## How valet storage works

With valet storage, the provider does the carrying. Our operations team collects your items from home, stores them and delivers them back on a date that suits you. You never need to visit a facility, and you do not need to arrange a vehicle.

The benefit is convenience: someone else handles the heavy lifting and the logistics. The trade-off is that you do not have walk-in access, because items are requested and delivered.

## The real cost of self-storage

The unit rent is only part of the cost. When you add everything up, self-storage often involves:

- Transport: a van or a mover, often for two trips, one in and one out
- Helpers: friends or hired labour to carry heavy furniture
- Packing materials, such as boxes, tape and blankets
- Your own time, which can be a full weekend or more
- A second trip, if the unit turns out to be too small or too large
- Parking and other incidental costs

For a household move, these extras often tilt the balance.

## The real cost of valet storage

For valet storage, the main costs are the storage itself and the service, usually quoted together. It helps to ask what is included:

- Collection from your home, and how many people and what equipment are used
- Wrapping and protection for furniture
- Storage on pallets or in crates
- Delivery back to you, and how that is scheduled
- Any extras, such as stair carry or long push

We price from your inventory, explain what could change the number, and confirm extras in advance.

## Where each tends to win

Self-storage fits you if:

- You want to visit your things whenever you like
- You have a vehicle, strong helpers and time to spare
- You are storing frequently used items, such as business stock you handle weekly
- You want to arrange your items yourself

Valet storage fits you if:

- You do not own a car, or the lift and loading logistics are difficult
- You are packing up a whole home and do not want to make multiple trips
- You are short of time, or the move is tied to a deadline such as key handover or the end of a tenancy
- You have large or heavy items that are difficult to move
- You want pickup, storage and delivery handled by one team

## The honest trade-off: access

With valet storage, you request items back and we schedule delivery. Customers typically give three to five days' notice, which suits most households but not someone who needs spontaneous daily access. If you often need to pop in for one item, self-storage is simpler.

## How often will you really need your things?

A useful exercise is to estimate how often you will need something from storage:

- Rarely, maybe once or twice in the whole period: valet storage usually makes more sense
- Occasionally, a few times over several months: valet works well if you can plan ahead
- Every week or every few days: a nearby self-storage unit may be better

## Moving in and out

With self-storage, you need a plan for moving everything in, and out again. If you are moving a whole home, that can mean a day of heavy work, and another at the end. With valet storage, the moves are handled for you. Our rate for a full 14ft truckload is S$350, roughly the contents of a 3-room HDB including the living room, with part loads and specialist items quoted separately.

## Flexibility and length of stay

Think about how long you need storage and how likely your plans are to change:

- For short stays, a flexible arrangement matters more than the lowest rate
- For longer stays, compare the total cost over the whole period, not just the monthly figure
- If your plans are uncertain, ask about extensions and early endings before you commit

## Security and handling

Whichever you choose, ask about security and how items are handled. In self-storage, your own lock is the main protection. In valet storage, ask how items are handled, recorded and separated from other customers' belongings. In our case, items are placed on pallets or in crates, wrapped and kept separate from other customers' belongings.

## Environment

Many self-storage facilities run air-conditioning around the clock, which uses a lot of energy. Most of our space runs at ambient temperature, which keeps energy use lower, while a small share is climate-controlled for items that need it.

## A simple decision guide

Ask yourself:

- Do I have a vehicle and help to move things?
- How much time do I have, and how much is my time worth?
- How often will I need my belongings?
- Is my move tied to a deadline?
- Do I have heavy or awkward items?

If you answered "no" to the first and "rarely" to the third, valet storage will probably serve you better. If you want to be in your storage every week, choose self-storage. Either way, compare the full cost, not just the headline rate.
