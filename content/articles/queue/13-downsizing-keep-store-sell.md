Downsizing is about more than space. It often follows a life change, such as children moving out, a new job, retirement or a decision to simplify. The practical side can feel overwhelming because every item carries a decision, and many carry memories too.

This guide offers a method that keeps the process manageable, with clear steps, tests for difficult decisions and advice on selling, donating and storing.

## Start with the new home, not the old one

Before you sort anything, understand what you are moving into:

- Measure every room, including ceiling height, doorways, lifts and corridors
- Note the built-in storage, such as wardrobes, cabinets and shelves
- Sketch a simple floor plan and where the key pieces of furniture would go
- Check what the new home already includes, such as appliances

The aim is to fit your life in the new space, not to squeeze in everything you own.

## Set a target

Decide how much you want to keep. A simple way to set a target is to count the storage you will have, and aim to fill no more than about three quarters of it. Leaving space makes a small home feel comfortable, rather than crowded.

## Sort in four groups, one room at a time

Work room by room, and put each item into one of four groups:

- Keep: used regularly and fits the new home
- Store: not needed now but worth keeping, such as seasonal items, sentimental pieces or furniture for a later move
- Sell: valuable items in good condition that you will not use
- Donate or recycle: everything else

Start with the easiest rooms, such as the storeroom or the guest room, to build momentum. Leave the most emotional areas, such as photos and a child's bedroom, until later.

## Tests for difficult decisions

When you are unsure, try these questions:

- Have I used this in the past year?
- Would I buy this again at today's price?
- Does it fit the new home, in both size and style?
- Does it have real value, financial or emotional?
- Is it replaceable if I miss it later?

If the answer to most is no, the item probably belongs in the sell or donate pile.

## How to use storage wisely

The "store" group is the one people struggle with, because storage can become a place to delay decisions. To keep it useful:

- Keep only what you truly expect to use again
- Set a review date, for example six or twelve months, and release anything you have not missed
- Choose a compact amount of space and add more later if needed, rather than paying for space you do not use
- Keep an inventory with photos, so you can find things without opening everything

## Selling

Plan your sales early, because buyers need time and large items need collection:

- Photograph items in good light, and describe them honestly, including measurements and any flaws
- List large furniture first, since it takes longest to sell
- Use online marketplaces and community groups, and consider consignment for valuable items
- Set a deadline, and reduce prices or donate whatever is left
- Meet buyers safely, and be present when items are collected

## Donating and recycling

Many charities and community groups accept furniture, clothes, books and household goods, though each has its own rules. Book collections a couple of weeks ahead. Recycle electronics and batteries at proper collection points. For items that are broken or unsafe, arrange proper disposal rather than donating them.

## Sentimental items

Photos, letters and family heirlooms deserve gentle handling:

- Keep the best, and be willing to let go of duplicates
- Digitise photos, letters and documents, so a copy exists whatever happens
- Store physical items in sturdy boxes, with acid-free paper where you can
- Keep a small "memory box" for each family member, rather than keeping everything
- Photograph bulky sentimental items, such as a child's artwork or a large piece of furniture, before letting them go

## Furniture and fitting it in

For each large piece, measure it and check it will go through the doors, up the lift and into the room. Take notes on dimensions, because older furniture is often larger than you remember. If a piece does not fit, consider whether it could be adapted, such as a dining table with leaves removed, or whether it is time to say goodbye.

## Timing your move

A sensible timeline:

- Eight weeks before: measure, decide the target and start sorting
- Six weeks before: list items for sale and book donations
- Four weeks before: finish sorting, start packing the "keep" and "store" groups
- Two weeks before: confirm storage and moving dates, and finish selling
- One week before: pack essentials, label boxes by new room and clean

## A final thought

Downsizing is easier when you focus on what you want to keep, rather than what you are giving up. Taking it one room at a time, and one decision at a time, makes it manageable.
