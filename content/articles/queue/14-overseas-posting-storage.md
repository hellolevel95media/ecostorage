An overseas posting, long study stint or extended trip raises the same question: what happens to everything you own in Singapore? The answer is usually a mix of shipping, selling, storing and handing things to family.

This guide gives you a timeline, a way to decide what goes where and practical advice on preparing belongings for months or years in storage.

## Start with the big questions

Before you sort anything, answer a few things:

- How long will you be away, and how certain is that?
- Are you returning to the same home, or will you need a new one?
- Are you giving up a lease, or keeping a home you own?
- Will you rent out your home, and if so, will it be furnished?

These answers shape everything else. A one-year posting with a return to the same flat is very different from an open-ended move.

## Sort belongings into groups

Divide everything into groups:

- Travel with you or ship: things you will use daily abroad
- Store in Singapore: furniture, appliances, seasonal items and sentimental belongings you will want when you return
- Sell or donate: duplicates, items that are cheap to replace, and anything you would not miss
- Leave with family or a trusted person: documents and items you may need at short notice

A useful test is to compare the cost of storing and shipping an item with the cost of replacing it. Storing a sofa for three years may cost more than a new one. Storing a high-quality dining set or a family heirloom is usually worthwhile.

## A timeline

- Three months before: decide your plan, get quotes for shipping and storage, and start sorting
- Two months before: sell and donate, give notice on your lease or arrange tenants, and book storage
- One month before: pack, label and photograph everything, and close or transfer utilities
- One week before: confirm collection, finish packing essentials and give your contact details to a trusted person
- Departure: take your documents and valuables with you

## Preparing belongings for long-term storage

Nobody will check on things for months, so preparation matters more than usual:

- Clean everything, and make sure it is completely dry
- Use breathable covers instead of sealed plastic, particularly for fabric and upholstery
- Pack fragile and valuable items in sturdy boxes with plenty of padding
- Use moisture absorbers, such as silica gel, and replace them if storage will last a long time
- Choose enclosed crate storage for sensitive pieces such as artwork, instruments or electronics
- Clean and dry appliances, and leave doors slightly open

Our guide on mould explains how to prepare each type of material, which is particularly important for stays of a year or more.

## Special items

- Mattresses and upholstered furniture: vacuum, clean, dry completely and use breathable covers
- Leather: clean and condition, stuff bags with paper and store in dust bags
- Books and documents: store flat in acid-free boxes with moisture absorbers
- Clothes: wash, dry and pack in breathable boxes
- Wine and food: do not store perishables, and ask whether wine can be stored at all

## What not to store

Check what a provider will not accept. Perishables, flammable materials and hazardous goods are typically excluded, so clear them out before you leave. Also keep:

- Passports, IDs, banking documents and legal papers with you or a trusted person
- Jewellery and high-value items, unless you have arranged proper protection
- Medication
- Items you may need at short notice

## Keep an inventory

Make a list of everything in storage, with photos. Share it with someone you trust, so they can ask for the right items if needed. Number each box and keep the list somewhere safe, such as a cloud folder.

## Managing storage from abroad

Think about the practicalities:

- Set up payments that do not rely on you being in Singapore, such as automatic payments
- Nominate a contact who can authorise a delivery or answer questions
- Share account details and contact information with that person
- Check time zones, so you know when you can be reached
- Agree how you will update the provider if your return date changes

## Insurance

Check whether your own home or contents insurance covers belongings in storage, and whether it applies when you are overseas. Ask the storage provider what protection applies. Keep copies of receipts and valuations for expensive items.

## Plan your return

Think about what you will need when you land:

- How much notice is needed for returns, so your belongings are waiting when you arrive
- Which items you need first, such as beds and kitchen essentials
- Where your belongings will be delivered, and who will receive them if you are delayed
- Whether you will need to extend storage if your plans change

## Flexibility matters

Postings change. If your return date moves, you want to extend easily. Ask about extensions, price changes over time and notice periods. We schedule deliveries on a date that suits you, and a few days' notice is usually enough.

## A short checklist

- Decide what to ship, store, sell and leave with family
- Clean, dry and pack carefully, with breathable covers and moisture absorbers
- Keep an inventory with photos and share it
- Arrange payments and a nominated contact
- Plan your return and ask about extensions

A little planning now means your belongings will be in the same condition when you come back.
