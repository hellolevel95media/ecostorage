Most moving-day stress comes from boxes that are too heavy, too full or impossible to identify. A few simple rules fix almost all of it, and they apply whether you are moving house, storing belongings or packing up an office.

This guide covers choosing boxes, packing room by room, taping and filling, labelling, and keeping an inventory.

## Start with the right boxes and materials

Good packing starts with the right supplies:

- Boxes of several sizes, including small ones for heavy items
- Strong packing tape and a tape dispenser
- Packing paper, bubble wrap and soft padding such as towels
- Markers and labels
- Stretch film for furniture
- Wardrobe boxes for hanging clothes
- Scissors, a box cutter and a notebook

Use sturdy boxes in good condition. Old boxes that are soft or damp can fail at the bottom. If you reuse boxes, check them carefully.

## Match the box size to the contents

Heavy items such as books, tinned food and tools belong in small boxes. Light items such as bedding, cushions and soft toys go in large ones. A big box full of books is difficult to lift and can split at the bottom.

## Keep weights manageable

A useful guide is to keep boxes to a weight one person can lift comfortably. If you are straining, the box is too heavy. As a rough guide, many people aim to keep ordinary boxes below about 15 to 20 kilograms, and lighter for older helpers or long carries. Heavy boxes are also harder to stack safely, in storage or on a truck.

## Pack firmly, but not to bursting

- Fill gaps with paper or soft clothing, so nothing shifts
- Put heavier items at the bottom and lighter ones on top
- Close boxes flat, with no bulging flaps
- Do not overfill, since boxes that bulge cannot be stacked safely

Shake the box gently. If you can hear things moving, add more padding.

## Tape properly

Tape the bottom along the seam, then add a couple of extra strips across the sides, particularly for heavy boxes. Close the top the same way. Taping along the edges as well helps prevent flaps opening when boxes are stacked.

## Pack room by room

Packing one room at a time keeps things organised:

- Start with items you use least, such as seasonal decorations and spare linens
- Pack kitchen items carefully: plates on their edges, glasses wrapped individually, and heavy pots at the bottom
- Keep bedroom items together, with a separate box for bedding you will need first
- Pack bathroom items in sealed bags, to avoid leaks
- Keep an essentials box, with items such as toiletries, a change of clothes, chargers, medication and basic tools

## Packing fragile items

Wrap each item individually in packing paper or bubble wrap, and pack items snugly with no space to move. Place heavier items at the bottom and keep fragile items on top. Mark the box fragile on more than one side, and keep it upright. Plates travel best standing on their edges, like records, rather than flat.

## What should never share a box

Some things should never be mixed:

- Heavy and fragile items
- Liquids and anything that could leak, unless sealed in a bag and a container
- Items that might damage others, such as sharp tools with clothes
- Damp or dirty items with anything clean
- Anything valuable or irreplaceable with general items

## Items to carry yourself

Documents, passports, valuables, medication and electronics with sensitive data are best carried by you or kept in a separate labelled bag. Keep keys, chargers and essentials accessible.

## Labelling

Labelling is where people save the most time. A simple system:

- Write on the side of the box, not the top, because boxes get stacked
- Include the destination room, a short description of the contents, and a number
- Mark fragile boxes clearly
- Use a different colour marker or coloured tape for each room
- Write "this way up" on boxes that need to stay upright

For storage, add your name or a reference.

## Keep an inventory

Number each box and keep a simple list of what each number contains. A spreadsheet or a note on your phone works well. Take photos of the contents of important boxes before sealing. If you are storing items, keep a copy somewhere other than your phone, and share it with someone you trust.

## Packing furniture and large items

Dismantle what you can, and keep screws and fittings in a labelled bag taped to the furniture. Wrap surfaces with moving blankets or stretch film, and protect corners with cardboard. Cover mattresses with a breathable bag, and stand sofas on their ends if they fit.

## Protect against moisture

In Singapore's climate, make sure everything is clean and completely dry before it goes into a box. Add silica gel to boxes holding clothes, books and electronics, and avoid sealed plastic for fabric.

## Packing timeline

- Four weeks before: gather supplies, declutter and start with items you rarely use
- Two weeks before: pack most rooms, label and number boxes
- One week before: pack the kitchen and bedrooms, keep an essentials box
- The day before: pack the last items, finish the inventory and prepare an access plan
