If you run a small business or sell online, stock tends to take over your home, your office or your storeroom. Every extra carton costs you space you are already paying a lot for, and a crowded workspace slows everything down.

Off-site storage is a way to separate the stock you handle every day from the stock you only need occasionally. That shift can free up room, tidy your workspace and keep your records and equipment safer. This guide explains what businesses typically store, how to organise it, and how to compare options.

## What businesses commonly store off-site

- Seasonal and bulk inventory, such as festive stock or items bought in volume to lower the unit cost
- Event materials, display stands, banners, signage and pop-up gear
- Samples, prototypes and returned goods
- Records and documents that must be kept but are rarely opened
- Old equipment and furniture between office moves
- Packaging materials, such as boxes, mailers and labels, bought in bulk
- Marketing materials and merchandise

## Decide what belongs close and what can wait

The most useful question is how often you need access:

- Stock that ships out every few days belongs close to where you work
- Stock you need monthly or seasonally is a good candidate for storage with scheduled delivery
- Items you need once or twice a year, such as event gear and archives, are ideal for storage

Walk through your space and sort items into those three groups. You will probably find that a surprising amount of what takes up room is rarely touched.

## Plan for peak seasons

Online sellers in Singapore often see demand peak around well-known sales events, such as double-digit shopping days, year-end sales and festive periods like Chinese New Year. Plan your stock cycle around them:

- Move off-season stock into storage early, to free up room for fast sellers
- Schedule deliveries of peak-season stock to your workspace a few weeks before the rush
- Book deliveries in advance, because everyone else is busy at the same time
- Review what sold, and what remained, after each peak, so next year's order is better judged

## Organise before you move anything

A little organisation saves a lot of time:

- Label cartons with a product code or a range of codes, rather than only a product name
- Keep a spreadsheet matching each carton number to its contents, quantity and condition
- Group similar items in the same cartons and keep cartons a consistent size
- Mark the carton with the date it went into storage
- Take photos of the cartons, so you can identify them without opening them

When you ask for a delivery, quoting the carton number saves time and prevents mistakes.

## Cartons or pallets?

Pallets suit larger quantities. Bundled and wrapped, they keep items off the floor, separate from other customers' goods and easy to retrieve in full. Smaller businesses can start with a few modules and add more as stock grows. Think about how you will retrieve items:

- If you need whole pallets at a time, pallet storage is efficient
- If you need individual cartons at irregular times, label clearly and keep an inventory
- If items are sensitive to handling, such as electronics or fragile goods, ask about crate storage

## Records and documents

Records need extra thought. Keep an index of what is in each box, with the date range and a review date. Group files by type and year. Check with your accountant on how long different records must be kept, since requirements vary by type of record and business. Keep sensitive documents in sealed, labelled boxes, and ask how the provider handles confidential material.

## Protect your stock

Whatever you store, prepare it well:

- Keep stock clean and completely dry before packing
- Use sturdy cartons, and do not overfill them
- Use moisture absorbers for items that are sensitive to humidity, such as paper goods, fabrics and electronics
- Wrap fragile goods individually
- Keep an up-to-date count, and check it against your sales

## Compare your options

Businesses often consider several routes. A few questions help you compare:

- Renting a self-storage unit: you carry and arrange everything, and usually need access during opening hours
- Renting a small warehouse or shop space: more control, but higher cost and commitment
- Using a third-party fulfilment service: handles picking and shipping, but is a different service with its own costs
- Using valet-style storage: we collect, store and deliver on request, which suits stock you need occasionally

Compare the total cost, including transport, labour, your time and any minimum commitments. Ask about flexibility, because stock levels change over the year.

## Pricing is simpler than people expect

Storage is charged by space and duration, with delivery arranged when you need it. Because we collect from your premises, you do not need a vehicle or helpers. We price from your inventory, explain what could change the number, and confirm extras in advance.

## Questions to ask any provider

- How is space measured and priced?
- How much notice is needed for deliveries and retrievals?
- Can I retrieve part of my stock?
- What are the fees for access, handling and rescheduling?
- How are items recorded, handled and kept separate from other customers?
- What happens if there is damage?
- How easy is it to increase or reduce my space?
