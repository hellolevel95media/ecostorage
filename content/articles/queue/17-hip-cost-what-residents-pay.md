When the HIP notice arrives, the first question most households ask is how much it will cost. The answer has two parts: the programme itself, which is heavily subsidised, and the costs that sit around it, which HDB does not cover. Knowing both helps you budget properly instead of being surprised halfway through.

## The programme is split into essential and optional works

HIP bundles its works into packages. The Essential Improvements deal with public health and safety in ageing flats. They include repairing spalling concrete, replacing waste and soil discharge stacks, and replacing pipe sockets with a new clothes drying rack. For Singapore Citizen households, the Government funds these in full, and owners cannot opt out of them.

The Optional Improvements are a menu of upgrades to your own flat, such as toilet upgrading or new fittings. You choose which items to take, and you can decline all of them. Only owners who take the optional items pay a share of the cost.

Senior-friendly fittings under the EASE programme are offered alongside HIP. The Government pays most of that cost for Singapore Citizen households, and the share depends on flat type.

## How the co-payment works

For Singapore Citizen households, the owner's share of the optional package runs from 5% to 12.5% of the cost, depending on flat type, and the Government pays the rest. Smaller flat types pay the lowest percentage. Permanent residents and other households pay a higher share, so check the table that comes with your own notice.

Payment is only required after all the works are complete, so you do not need to put money down before your flat is touched. The exact dollar figures change when HDB updates its schedule, so read the current figures in your HDB information booklet or letter rather than relying on a number you saw in an older article or forum post.

## The costs HDB does not cover

This is where budgets tend to go wrong. The programme pays for the works, but your household carries everything around them:

- Cleaning after the works finish, including washing walls, floors and every surface the dust reached.
- Professional cleaning or laundering for fabric items, curtains and mattresses that were exposed.
- Replacing items damaged by dust, water or knocks, particularly electronics and anything left uncovered.
- Meals and incidental costs if the kitchen is out of use for a few days.
- Accommodation, if you decide to stay elsewhere while works are underway.
- Storage for furniture and belongings you move out of the flat.

None of these are large on their own, but together they can reach a few hundred dollars or more, and they are entirely in your control.

## Where storage fits in the budget

Storage is the one optional cost that can reduce other costs. A flat that is emptied of furniture needs far less cleaning afterwards, and nothing in it can be damaged. Items that stay in the unit need plastic covering and still pick up dust, and a bed or sofa that picks up renovation dust may need professional cleaning.

If you are deciding whether to store, compare the cost of storage against the cost of cleaning, replacing and worrying about everything left in place. Large furniture, mattresses and electronics are the usual candidates, because they cost the most to clean or replace. Smaller items can often stay in a sealed room.

## A simple budget to work from

Before the works start, list these four lines and put a rough number next to each:

- Optional improvements you plan to take, from your HDB letter.
- Cleaning after the works, whether you do it yourself or hire help.
- Storage or temporary accommodation, if any.
- A buffer for replacing or repairing something that gets damaged.

Adding a buffer matters more than it sounds. Works at this scale always turn up small surprises, and having a modest sum set aside turns them into minor annoyances.

## Decisions that save money later

Take your time with the optional items. Check whether your toilet passes the water test, since that can decide whether you need the toilet upgrade at all. Ask neighbours who have been through it which items they were glad they took. And decide about storage early, because a few weeks' notice gives you more choice of dates than a few days.

Done well, HIP costs a household very little compared with the value it adds to an older flat. The bills that catch people out are rarely the ones HDB sends. They are the cleaning, the replacements and the last-minute arrangements, and each of them is easier to handle when planned in advance.
