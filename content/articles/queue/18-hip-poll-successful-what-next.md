A successful HIP poll feels like the end of a process, but for residents it is the start of a long runway. Between the announcement and the day works begin in your flat, there are decisions to make, deadlines to meet and months of lead time you can use well. Knowing the sequence helps you spend that time on the right things.

## The timeline is longer than most people expect

HDB's information booklet describes a typical HIP project as taking around 18 months from the announcement of a successful poll, depending on site conditions. Works on the first block in a precinct usually begin about four months after the poll, and the remaining blocks follow in turn. Your own block may therefore be early or late in that window.

The work inside your flat is much shorter. HDB's guidance puts it at roughly 10 working days per unit, with works in common areas such as corridors and pipes running across a longer period. The programme also carries a one-year defects liability period after completion, so problems that appear in the first year can be reported and fixed.

## Choosing your optional items

Soon after the announcement, HDB invites owners to select their optional and senior-friendly items. The selection period runs for a limited number of weeks, and your letter states the exact dates. Treat that deadline seriously, since it affects both what goes into your flat and what you pay.

Before you decide, work through these questions:

- Does your toilet pass the water test? If it does, the toilet upgrade becomes a choice rather than a necessity.
- Are any items genuinely useful to your household, or are you taking them because they are subsidised?
- Do any of the items extend the time your flat is disrupted?
- Would an elderly family member benefit from the senior-friendly fittings?

Your letter and HDB's e-service pages hold the current details, so use them as the final word.

## Use the first few months to declutter

The lead time is the best opportunity you will get to clear your flat. Most households own far more than they use, and every item you remove before HIP is one you do not have to cover, move or clean after.

Start with the categories that are easy to decide: duplicate kitchenware, old clothes, outgrown toys, paperwork you no longer need and electronics you have replaced. Selling, donating or recycling these now means a smaller, simpler job when the notice for your own unit arrives.

## Decide what stays, what goes and what is stored

By the time your unit's schedule is confirmed, you should know which of your belongings fall into each of three groups:

- Items that stay in the flat in a protected room.
- Items that you will move out temporarily.
- Items that you will sell or donate.

The second group is usually where the planning takes time. Large furniture, mattresses and electronics are typical candidates, and storage needs a booked date. Arranging collection a few weeks before the works start leaves room to adjust if your contractor's schedule shifts.

## Prepare your household

Households run more smoothly through HIP when everyone knows what to expect. Talk to family members about the 10 working days, particularly anyone who works from home, studies at home or has health needs that make noise and dust harder to cope with. Think about where each person will be during the working day, and whether a relative's place or a quiet library might be an easier choice for some of those days.

Pets and young children need the same thought. A dusty, noisy flat with workers moving in and out is a hard place for either, and arranging care in advance removes a source of stress.

## What to do in the final fortnight

In the last two weeks before your works date, shift from planning to doing:

- Confirm the date and any access arrangements with the contractor.
- Back up computers and phones, and pack electronics away from the work areas.
- Clear and clean the room that will serve as your safe space.
- Photograph your flat and valuables for your own records.
- Confirm your storage collection, if you are using it.

## Keep the paperwork close

Keep your HDB letter, your selected items and the contractor's details together in one folder, physical or digital. If an issue turns up during works or within the defects liability period, having the original selections and dates to hand makes any follow-up much faster.

The households that handle HIP best are rarely the ones with the most time. They are the ones that used the months after the announcement to decide early, clear out and book what needed booking, so that the works themselves became the smaller part of the project.
