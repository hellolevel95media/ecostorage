HIP is a short project by renovation standards, with works in each unit taking around 10 working days according to HDB's guidance. For most adults, that is a tiring fortnight. For seniors, babies, toddlers and pets, it can be much harder. Dust, drilling and a stream of workers through the front door affect the people and animals who cannot simply put on headphones and carry on.

Planning around their needs makes the difference between a manageable fortnight and a stressful one.

## Start with the people who are most affected

Make a list of every person and pet in the flat, and write next to each one what the works will make harder for them. A grandparent with a respiratory condition needs clean air. A baby needs quiet for naps. A dog needs somewhere calm and a way to relieve itself that does not involve open doors and strangers.

Seeing these needs written down turns a vague worry into a list of problems you can solve one at a time.

## Seniors: air, access and routine

Dust is the biggest concern for elderly family members, especially anyone with asthma, COPD or heart conditions. Hacking and drilling release fine dust that drifts through gaps in doors and into every room. For seniors, spending the working day somewhere else is often the healthiest option.

Think through these points:

- A relative's home, a community centre or a quiet public space can serve as a daytime base.
- Medication, glasses, hearing aids and mobility aids should travel with the person, not stay in the flat.
- If the household member stays home, set up one sealed room with a bed, chair, water and a fan or air purifier.
- Check toilet access. If the bathroom is out of use for part of the works, arrange an alternative in advance.
- Keep walkways clear. Workers carry tools and materials, and a clear path prevents trips.

HDB offers senior-friendly fittings through the EASE programme alongside HIP, and installing them during the works means the flat becomes easier to live in afterwards.

## Babies and young children

Noise and dust affect children differently from adults. Babies sleep poorly in loud, dusty rooms, and toddlers are curious about workers, tools and anything lying around.

Practical steps to take:

- Arrange daytime care elsewhere for the loudest days, if you can.
- Pack away toys, books and small items that could pick up dust or get lost.
- Put gates or closed doors between children and any work area.
- Wash hands and faces more often than normal, since dust settles on everything.
- Remove cot mattresses and soft furnishings, or seal them in plastic in the safe room.

Children also settle better when they know what to expect. A short explanation of who the workers are and why they are in the flat helps younger ones cope.

## Pets

Pets pick up on stress quickly. Loud noises, unfamiliar people and an open front door create anxiety, and some animals bolt when a door is left ajar.

- Board the pet or arrange a stay with a friend or relative for the working days, if you can.
- If the pet stays, keep it in a closed room with food, water, bedding and a familiar toy.
- Tell the workers the pet is inside, and ask them to keep that door shut.
- Keep litter trays and feeding areas clean, as dust gets into everything.
- Make sure ID tags and microchip details are up to date, in case an animal slips out.

Small animals such as hamsters, birds and fish need particular care. Dust and noise stress them, and their enclosures need covering or relocating.

## Create one protected room

Whatever the mix of people in your household, one room set up properly serves as the foundation of the whole plan. Choose the room furthest from the work areas, clear it of everything you do not need, and seal the doorway with tape and plastic. Put in a bed or mattress, a fan, charging points, water and anything the household uses daily.

Furniture that would normally go in that room often ends up crowded into it, which defeats the purpose. Moving larger items out of the flat altogether leaves the protected room as a room, not a store.

## Plan the days, not just the room

The works are spread across 10 working days, and some are noisier than others. Ask your contractor which days involve the heaviest work, and schedule the most vulnerable family members and pets elsewhere on those days. Keep the rest of the time as normal as possible, with regular meals and sleep.

A household that plans like this comes out of HIP with a freshly upgraded flat and a routine that held together. If clearing furniture from the flat would make your plan easier, our team can collect it, store it and bring it back once the works are done.
