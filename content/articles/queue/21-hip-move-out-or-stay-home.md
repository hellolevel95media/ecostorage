Almost everyone in an HDB block going through HIP faces the same choice: tolerate the works at home, or leave for the duration. There is no single right answer. The best choice depends on who lives in your flat, how much you can work around the disruption and what an alternative would cost.

## What staying home really involves

HDB's guidance puts works in each unit at about 10 working days. During that time, workers come and go, water and power may be cut off for stretches, and the toilet or kitchen can be out of use for part of the works. The noise starts in the morning and the dust travels through the whole flat.

Households that stay usually manage by setting up one protected room, spending daytime hours elsewhere and living simply for the period. It is workable, and many families do it. It works best when the household is small, healthy and flexible, with at least one person who can be home to manage access.

## Signs that leaving makes sense

Certain circumstances push the decision clearly towards moving out, even temporarily:

- A household member with respiratory problems, a chronic illness or limited mobility.
- Infants or very young children who need regular naps and clean air.
- Someone working from home on calls or deadlines that cannot move.
- Students sitting exams during the works.
- A flat that is full of furniture and has no space for a safe room.

Even in these cases, leaving does not need to mean a full relocation. Spending daytime hours away, or sending the most affected person to stay with relatives for the heaviest days, can solve most of the problem.

## The options if you do leave

A few arrangements work well for HIP:

- Stay with family or friends for the 10 working days.
- Book a short-term rental or serviced apartment for the period.
- Use a hotel for the noisiest days only.
- Split the household, with some members out and some at home.

Each option has a cost, so compare them against how much comfort you gain. Staying with relatives costs little in money but asks a lot of the hosts. A short-term rental gives privacy and costs more.

## Staying home but moving the belongings

There is a middle path that suits many households: stay home, but move the bulky furniture out. A flat without wardrobes, beds and sofas is easier to work in, simpler to clean afterwards and much safer for your belongings. Workers also move through it faster, because they are not working around obstacles.

The items that gain the most from storage are the ones that are hardest to protect in place:

- Mattresses, sofas and upholstered chairs.
- Wardrobes, cabinets and bookshelves.
- Televisions, computers and other electronics.
- Anything delicate or valuable.

With those out, you can keep a bed or a mattress in a sealed room for sleeping and live simply for a fortnight. Your belongings return when the works finish, and the flat is easier to clean because the heavy items were never in the dust.

## Comparing the costs

The money question usually decides it, so put real figures on each route:

- Staying home with no changes: little direct cost, but more cleaning, more risk to belongings and more daily stress.
- Staying home with storage: our price is S$350 per full 14ft truckload, roughly a 3-room HDB's worth including the living room. Part loads and specialist items are quoted separately, and any extras are communicated in advance.
- Moving out for the works: the cost of accommodation for the period, plus the cost of storing or protecting what you leave behind.

For many households, storage without moving out gives most of the benefit for a small share of the cost.

## Questions to settle the decision

Before committing, answer these honestly:

- Who in the household is most affected by dust and noise?
- Can everyone live without the toilet or kitchen for a short period?
- Is there somewhere else to spend the working hours?
- How much of the furniture is hard to protect in place?
- What is the budget for the fortnight?

If the answers point to one or two vulnerable people, send those people out. If they point to the furniture, send the furniture out. If neither, staying with a protected room is a reasonable plan.

## Whichever you pick, start early

The decision matters less than how soon you make it. Rentals, relatives and storage slots all get harder to arrange as the works date nears. Settle the plan a few weeks ahead, confirm the dates with your contractor and let everyone in the household know what to expect. The works run for a short time, and a clear plan turns them into an inconvenience you manage rather than a disruption that manages you.
